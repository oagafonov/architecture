Toward Evidence-Based Architectural Decision Making Over Fragmented Knowledge

Summary
Architectural design involves decisions that should be justified by explicit rationale and grounded in documented evidence. This paper proposes an evidence-based architectural decision-support framework that preserves document-level requirement-architecture relations as contextualized evidence. The framework stores concrete requirement-architecture relations with explicit provenance in a knowledge graph (KG) and recombines them at analysis time for a given design question. Large language models (LLMs) support KG construction, exploration, and summarization, while humans make the final judgments in a human-on-the-loop setting. We demonstrate the framework through an automotive case study comparing centralized and decentralized electronic control unit architectures. The results show an approximately 80% reduction in analysis time, the discovery of up to ten additional comparison points from domain-agnostic literature, and the highest agreement with expert judgments when consolidating domain-specific evidence, while maintaining traceability to source documents. These findings suggest that preserving and recombining fragmented architectural evidence can support transparent, evidence-backed decision making.
